RA Renée Alvarez
Project Manager, Store Operations · 9 years · PMP, CSM
Today's task: opening a project to put a private-label patio line into 40 more stores before the spring reset.

Step 1 of 6 · Getting authority

So I've just been handed the patio expansion, and the first thing I do is not plan anything — I write a charter, which is the one-page document that formally says this project exists, who is paying for it, and who is allowed to say no to it. Because I hold a PMP — the Project Management Professional credential from the Project Management Institute — I'm working out of a reference called the PMBOK Guide, which files everything I'm doing right now under a phase it calls Initiating; the whole point of that phase is to get authority in writing before I spend anybody else's time.

Practically, that means the next hour goes to naming stakeholders: merchandising, who owns what we sell; supply chain, who owns whether forty stores' worth of furniture can physically show up; store operations, who owns the payroll hours to build the display; and finance, who eats the markdown if none of it sells. Those four go into a stakeholder register, and I score each one on how much power they have against how much they care — a two-by-two you'll hear called the power–interest grid — because the person with a lot of power and no interest is the one who quietly kills this in week six.
